\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Motif-typed $Z$ surfaces the bias that random $Z$ hides}\label{sec:rho_max}

We first quantified the geometric content of the bias-diagnostic
$\rho_{\max}$ on the 1000~Genomes Project chr22 panel
($n=3{,}202$ samples; $30$--$35$\,Mb window; $17{,}021$ MAF$\geq$0.05
SNPs). For each of $30$ random interaction-feature matrices $Z$
($m=100$ columns of two-way SNP$\times$SNP products), we computed
$\rho_{\max}$ for $100$ target SNPs (3{,}000 measurements per cell).
We compared two $Z$ constructions $\times$ two target-SNP pools:

\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Random $Z$}: columns are products of randomly-paired SNPs
      (Yelmen \emph{et~al.}\ 2026 baseline).
\item \textbf{Motif-typed $Z$}: columns are products of \emph{same-gene} (P1)
      pairs drawn from the GraphGWAS chr22 cache
      ($33{,}843$ unique pairs across $95$ multi-variant genes).
\item \textbf{Null targets}: $11{,}035$ SNPs with no annotated same-gene
      partner (biology-isolated).
\item \textbf{Causal targets}: $5{,}986$ SNPs with $\geq 1$ same-gene
      partner (biology-aligned, hence in the $Z_{\mathrm{motif}}$ span by construction).
\end{itemize}

The four-cell comparison (Fig.~3 in the List of Display Items;
\texttt{paper/epistasis\_v1/figures/fig3\_motif\_z\_causal\_arm}) shows
that the geometry of $Z$ separates the two target pools far more sharply
when $Z$ is biology-typed:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Target pool          & $\rho_{\max}$ random $Z$ & $\rho_{\max}$ motif $Z$ & $\Delta$\\
\midrule
Null (biology-isolated) & 0.545 & 0.468 & $+0.077$\\
Causal (biology-aligned) & 0.578 & \textbf{0.670} & $-0.092$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

KS tests confirm both within-pool distributions differ (null:
$D=0.236$, $p=1.85\times10^{-73}$; causal: $D=0.261$,
$p=5.73\times10^{-90}$). The crucial direction is that random $Z$
\emph{under}-estimates $\rho_{\max}$ for biology-aligned target SNPs:
the random-$Z$ column space contains arbitrary directions that overlap
with target SNPs through generic Gram-matrix noise, but it does not span
the actual interaction direction. Motif-typed $Z$ spans the small
biology-aligned subspace that \emph{contains} the true interaction
direction, surfacing the bias by $+0.092$. The within-construction swing
between causal and null targets is $0.670-0.468=0.202$ for motif $Z$
versus $0.578-0.545=0.033$ for random $Z$ -- motif-typed $Z$ is a
$\sim$$6\times$ more sensitive bias diagnostic for distinguishing
biology-aligned from biology-isolated SNPs. At
$\lambda_{\mathrm{var}}=0.1, n=3{,}202$, the cell-mean shifts $\mu$
are $\mu_{\mathrm{causal,motif}}=12.27$ versus
$\mu_{\mathrm{causal,random}}=10.52$: random $Z$ would falsely report
the SNP as $1.7\sigma$ closer to the null than its actual position.

\textbf{Implication}: Yelmen \emph{et~al.}'s closed-form bias diagnostic
is conservative when applied with random $Z$, in the sense that it
\emph{understates} the bias for the very SNPs where omitted-interaction
contamination matters most. A genome-wide bias audit should be run with
a graph-typed $Z$.

\subsection{Power versus classical exhaustive baselines}\label{sec:power_baselines}

We benchmarked the GraphGWAS toolkit (M1 LD-pruned co-occurrence; M2
motif-filtered) against three classical-baseline references on the
yeast Scenario~A panel from §\ref{sec:yeast_validation}:

\begin{description}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item[BOOST]  PLINK2~v2.0.0-a.6.5LM \emph{drops} the
  \texttt{--epistasis} flag (was PLINK~1.9-only). The closest in-toolkit
  equivalent is M1 with LD-pruning disabled, which is the
  Wan~2010-style exhaustive interaction-LR screen.
\item[MAPIT]  Crawford-lab marginal-epistasis test (Rcpp). The
  \texttt{run\_mapit} R-template wrapper is staged but not deployed
  ($\Rightarrow$ deferred for next iteration; documented in
  \texttt{graphgwas.epistasis\_baselines}).
\item[MDR]    \texttt{scikit-mdr v0.4.6} (multifactor dimensionality
  reduction; classifier-based). Pipelined with logistic regression
  because \texttt{MDR} is a transformer in scikit-mdr, not a classifier.
\end{description}

On the yeast Scenario~A panel ($n=1{,}011$ samples; $6{,}625$ SNPs on
chr9$/$10$/$12; binarised at the median for MDR's case$\,/\,$control input;
5{,}000 random pairs $+$ the $10$ BCY1 $\times$ TPK1 cross-pairs
force-included), MDR gave 100\% finite scores under our stratified-3-fold
loop after the pipeline fix, but its CV balanced accuracy on the BCY1$\times$TPK1
canonical pair was $0.522$ -- barely above the chance level of $0.5$. The
ground-truth pair ranked $2{,}598$ of $5{,}010$ ($51.8$th percentile).

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Method                                & BCY1$\times$TPK1 percentile & Best score / $q$\\
\midrule
M2 motif-filtered (GraphGWAS)         & \textbf{1.2\%} (1{,}148\,/\,94{,}440) & $q=1.7\times10^{-5}$\\
M1 LD-pruned exhaustive (GraphGWAS)   & NF (LD-prune replaces canonical reps) & --\\
MDR (scikit-mdr v0.4.6)               & $51.8$\% (2{,}598\,/\,5{,}010) & CV bal-acc $0.522$\\
BOOST (plink2 v2)                     & UNAVAILABLE & --\\
MAPIT (R/Rscript)                     & DEFERRED & --\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

M2 outranks MDR by a factor of $\sim$$43\times$ in percentile rank. The
diagnostic mechanism is two-fold: (i) MDR's classifier-based scoring
is power-limited at low MAF on small panels (BCY1's representative has
MAF~$0.059$, TPK1's $0.071$, so 9-cell genotype-tables become sparse
under cross-validation); (ii) random pair sampling is statistically
intractable -- $5{,}000$ pairs out of the $6{,}625^2/2 \approx 22\,$M
pairwise space is $0.02$\% coverage, requiring biology-aware filtering
just to reach the candidate set in practice.

\subsection{Yeast real-data validation: BCY1$\times$TPK1 across three
phenotype regimes}\label{sec:yeast_validation}

We tested whether each of the five graph-native methods (M1--M5) recovers
the canonical cAMP-PKA epistatic pair BCY1 (YIL033C) $\times$ TPK1
(YJL164C) on the 1011 Yeast Genomes panel ($n=1{,}011$; $6{,}625$
chr9/10/12 SNPs at MAF$\geq 0.05$; $4{,}572$ in the multi-omics graph
cache). Three phenotype regimes target different methods:

\begin{description}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item[Scenario A]  $y = \beta\,(g_{\mathrm{BCY1}}-\bar g_{\mathrm{BCY1}})\,(g_{\mathrm{TPK1}}-\bar g_{\mathrm{TPK1}})+\varepsilon$, $\beta=3$ ($R^2_{\mathrm{causal}}\approx 10$\%) -- positive interaction, M1$/$M2$/$M5 turf.
\item[Scenario B]  Case$\,/\,$control with double-carriers forced into cases (baseline $P_{\mathrm{case}}=0.10$) -- M3 turf.
\item[Scenario C]  Synthetic-lethal depletion (double-carriers excluded from cases) -- M4 turf.
\end{description}

Recovery is gene-level: any (BCY1-variant $\times$ TPK1-variant) pair in
a method's result list counts as recovery. Total runtime $\approx 9$
minutes (3 scenarios $\times \approx 3$ min/scenario, no Neo4j).

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
Scenario                          & M1 & M2                 & M3 & M4 & M5\\
\midrule
A — positive interaction          & NF & \textbf{1{,}148} ($q=1.7\times10^{-5}$) & NF & NF & NF\\
B — case-enriched co-occurrence   & NF & \textbf{4{,}232} ($q=8.5\times10^{-7}$) & NF & NF & NF\\
C — synthetic-lethal depletion    & NF & 29{,}809 ($q\approx 1.0$) & NF & NF & NF\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{M2 motif-filtering is the only method that recovers BCY1\,$\times$\,TPK1
under signal regimes where a pairwise interaction-LR test has power}
(Scenarios A and B). M2 dominates in both quantitative
positive-interaction (A) and case-enriched co-occurrence (B), even
though regression on a binary phenotype is suboptimal in B. M2's rank
in the synthetic-lethal regime (C) is essentially random
($29{,}809/94{,}440$, $q\approx 1.0$) -- the regression-based test
correctly returns null for a depletion-only signal.

The other four methods miss for traceable reasons:
M1's position-based LD pruning replaces BCY1's representative with
chr9:282798 (intergenic) and TPK1's with YJL167W -- the kept LD-block
representatives are no longer BCY1$\,/\,$TPK1 by gene annotation; M3
returns 79{,}636 case$\,/\,$ctrl-edge classifications under
imbalanced $n_{\mathrm{case}}=119$ vs $n_{\mathrm{ctrl}}=892$, with
BCY1$\,\times\,$TPK1 missing from the case-unique edges due to the same
LD-prune-replacement issue; M4's $\min_{\mathrm{expected}}=0.5$ filter
combined with low MAFs gives expected co-occurrence $\approx 0.28$,
below the Poisson-tail threshold; M5 (variant-priority RWR) is
analysed in §\ref{sec:higher_order}.

\subsection{Higher-order methods: gene-priority RWR rescues M5}\label{sec:higher_order}

The §\ref{sec:yeast_validation} result that variant-priority M5
(\texttt{mutual\_rwr\_pair\_scores}) misses BCY1$\times$TPK1 across all
three scenarios is traceable to seed-pool sparsity: only $7$ of the
BCY1$\,/\,$TPK1 variants survive the MAF$\geq 0.05$ panel filter into the
seed pool, so mutual-RWR scoring among $4{,}572$ seeds does not push
the cross-pairs into the top-$2{,}000$.

We re-tested the §Y.5 hypothesis with a \emph{gene-priority} RWR
variant (\texttt{gene\_pair\_rwr\_scores}): seed the random walk on
gene nodes rather than variant nodes; rank gene pairs by mutual
stationary probability; expand each top gene pair into all
(BCY1-variant $\times$ TPK1-variant) cross-pairs and test each pair for
interaction. Two adjacency choices were compared:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Bipartite} (variant$\leftrightarrow$gene only): pure two-step
      walk variant$\to$gene$\to$variant. BCY1 and TPK1 share no annotated
      variant in our cache, so the bipartite RWR returns mutual score
      $\equiv 0$ for the pair. Failed.
\item \textbf{Heterograph} (gene$\leftrightarrow$gene weighted by shared
      pathways with IDF weighting plus PPI edges): $887$ genes that have
      at least one variant in the dosage panel; $265{,}304$ edges; max
      edge weight $10.14$. The cache's PPI annotation
      \emph{omits the canonical BCY1$\leftrightarrow$TPK1 edge} (BCY1
      lists itself as its only known partner -- a curation bug). When
      the canonical BioGRID cAMP-PKA edges are injected
      ($+3.0$ weight on BCY1$\leftrightarrow$TPK1, also TPK1$\leftrightarrow$TPK2/3
      and TPK pairwise), gene-priority RWR ranks BCY1$\times$TPK1 at gene-pair
      rank $770$ of $2{,}000$ (RWR score $8.5\times10^{-6}$). Variant
      expansion of the top-$2{,}000$ gene pairs yields $61{,}341$
      tested variant pairs; BCY1$\times$TPK1 surfaces at variant-pair rank
      $1{,}522$ with $p_{\mathrm{int}}=1.76\times10^{-10}$ and BH-FDR
      $q=1.08\times10^{-5}$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Implication}: graph-priority RWR succeeds where variant-priority
RWR fails, but only when the gene-gene graph carries the canonical
biological edge. The recovery is rate-limited by gene-gene graph
quality, not by the RWR machinery. Paper-#2 recommends explicit
graph-curation (BioGRID/STRING for PPI, KEGG/Reactome for pathways,
SGD/GO for gene-ontology) as a pre-method workflow step rather than
treating the cache as a fixed input. The mass of higher-order methods
(M6 GNN-attention, M7 persistent-homology) is deferred for paper-#2
revision; only the structurally-unique pairwise claim is here.
