\abstract{
Classical genome-wide association studies --- including state-of-the-art
linear mixed models such as REGENIE --- are anti-conservative when the
true genetic architecture involves epistasis but the analytical model
omits it. Yelmen \emph{et~al.}\ (2026) showed that the bias grows with
sample size, so biobank-scale GWAS is more vulnerable, not less.
We present GraphGWAS-Epistasis, a graph-native epistasis-detection
toolkit that exploits a typed multi-omics knowledge graph (genes,
tissue-specific eQTLs, pathways, protein--protein interactions) to
build a biology-typed interaction subspace. Five detection strategies
operate on this substrate: M1 LD-pruned co-occurrence with explicit
interaction tests; M2 motif-filtered pairs (same-gene, PPI-partner,
same-pathway); M3 differential subgraph analysis between cases and
controls; M4 dark-matter / synthetic-incompatibility detection; and M5
gene-priority random walk with restart on a heterogeneous variant-gene-
pathway-PPI graph. On 1000 Genomes chr22 ($n=3{,}202$), motif-typed
$Z$ resolves the $\rho_{\max}$ separation between biology-aligned and
biology-isolated SNPs ${\sim}6\times$ more sharply than the random $Z$
of Yelmen \emph{et~al.}, surfacing a mean bias-statistic shift
$\Delta\mu=+1.7\sigma$ that random $Z$ hides. On the 1011 Yeast Genomes
panel, M2 recovers the canonical cAMP-PKA epistatic pair BCY1$\times$TPK1
at rank 1{,}148 of 94{,}440 (q\,$=1.7\times10^{-5}$, top 1.2\%), out-ranking
the MDR classical baseline by $\sim$$43\times$ in percentile and
out-detecting M1 (NF), M3 (NF), M4 (NF), and the variant-priority M5
(NF). Gene-priority M5 RWR rescues the BCY1$\times$TPK1 case once the
canonical BCY1$\leftrightarrow$TPK1 BioGRID edge is added to the cache
(variant-pair rank 1{,}522, $p_{\mathrm{int}}=1.76\times10^{-10}$,
$q=1.08\times10^{-5}$), demonstrating that the bottleneck on
graph-priority methods is gene-gene graph curation, not the random-walk
machinery itself. The toolkit is released as part of the GraphGWAS
Python package; all benchmarks reproduce from a single command.
}
