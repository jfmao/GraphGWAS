\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Relational vs flat annotation priors}

\textbf{Flat-prior fine-mapping collapses relational structure to a
scalar.} In the SuSiE/FINEMAP/PolyFun family, biological annotations
enter as a per-variant weight vector $w \in \mathbb{R}^n$, averaged
across tissues and detached from the gene, pathway and interaction
context each variant belongs to. GraphGWAS instead represents this
context as a typed graph (Figure~\ref{fig:graph}): variants connect
to genes through $\texttt{HAS\_CONSEQUENCE}$ and tissue-specific
$\texttt{eQTL}$ edges, genes participate in pathways via
$\texttt{IN\_PATHWAY}$, and the gene layer is coupled within itself
by protein--protein $\texttt{INTERACTS\_WITH}$ edges. The graph loaded on the 1000 Genomes Phase~3 high-coverage cohort
\cite{1000genomes2015global,byrska2022high} carries 70.7~million
variants, 20{,}092 GENCODE~v47 genes\cite{frankish2023gencode},
43.2~million GTEx~v8 tissue eQTLs\cite{gtex2020}, 230{,}850 STRING
interactions at combined score~$\geq$~700\cite{szklarczyk2023string},
and 370{,}000 ENCODE cCRE regulatory elements\cite{encode2020expanded}
(full counts in Supplementary Table~S1). All
relational edges are first-class: a fine-mapping procedure can either
collapse them to a scalar prior (the flat-prior approach) or carry
them through the inference (the relational approach we develop
below).

\subsection*{Hierarchical belief propagation on a three-layer factor graph}

HBP converts the graph into iterated message passing across
variant, gene and pathway layers (Figure~\ref{fig:hbp}). Each
iteration runs an upward pass---variant beliefs aggregate to gene
scores weighted by eQTL strength, then diffuse across the gene layer
along STRING PPI edges at a 0.3 coupling, then aggregate to
pathway scores---followed by a downward pass that returns a variant
prior $\pi^{(t)}$ via the same edges. The variant belief is combined
as $b^{(t+1)} = \alpha \cdot \text{softmax}(u) + (1-\alpha) \pi^{(t)}$,
where $u$ is the LD-deconvolved z-statistic, and damped by
$\lambda$ before renormalisation (defaults $\alpha = 0.6$,
$\lambda = 0.5$, 5 rounds). Under positive damping the iteration is
a strict contraction on the probability simplex, so by Banach's
fixed-point theorem HBP converges geometrically to a unique
self-consistent belief (Theorem~2, Supplementary Note~S1). At
default hyperparameters the algorithm reaches four-decimal
convergence in $\approx$0.08~s per locus on 1000 Genomes chromosome~22,
approximately 20--30$\times$ faster than SuSiE and FINEMAP on the
same data.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{fig2_hbp_schematic.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Hierarchical belief propagation on a three-layer factor graph.}
HBP performs message passing on a typed factor graph with variant
nodes (bottom), gene nodes (middle), and pathway nodes (top). The
upward pass aggregates variant beliefs into gene scores weighted by
eQTL strength, diffuses them once along the STRING
protein--protein interaction graph at a fixed 0.3 coupling, and
aggregates to the pathway layer. The downward pass reverses the
direction and produces a variant prior $\pi^{(t)}$. Each iteration

$b^{(t+1)} = \alpha \cdot \text{softmax}(u) + (1-\alpha) \pi^{(t)}$
and damps by $\lambda$ before renormalisation. Defaults are
$\alpha = 0.6$, $\lambda = 0.5$, $T = 5$ rounds. Under positive
damping the update is a strict $\ell_1$ contraction on the
probability simplex (Theorem~2, Supplementary Note~S1), guaranteeing
geometric convergence to a unique fixed point. See
Methods~\ref{methods:hbp} for the full algorithm.}

\end{figure}

\subsection*{L1 Bayesian fine-mapping wins weak signal 27--2 against SuSiE}

The regime that matters most for post-GWAS discovery is the one
where statistical signal alone is insufficient to resolve the causal
variant: sub-genome-wide-significant loci, rare-variant effects, and
complex LD blocks. We designed L1 Bayesian fine-mapping for this
regime. L1 first LD-deconvolves the z-statistics
($u_i = z_i - \frac{1}{|N(i)|} \sum_{j \in N(i)} r^2_{ij} z_j$ with
$N(i) = \{j : r^2_{ij} > 0.3\}$), then combines the deconvolved
evidence with a multi-layer graph functional score $z_{\text{func}}$
via $s_i = \alpha u_i + (1-\alpha) z_{\text{func},i}$ with
$\alpha$ chosen by empirical Bayes on a held-out calibration set.
Under mild LD-decay assumptions the causal variant has the highest
unique-signal contribution (Theorem~3).

On 79 independent simulated loci with tissue-specific eQTL causal
variants and weak effect size ($\beta = 0.15$, $h^2 = 0.01$), L1
resolves 61/79 loci at rank~\#1 versus SuSiE's 45/79
(Figure~\ref{fig:weak_signal}). Mean causal-variant rank is 1.65
for L1 versus 2.84 for SuSiE. Head-to-head on matched replicates, L1
beats SuSiE on 27, ties on 50, and loses on 2 (win ratio 13.5\,:\,1,
sign-test $p < 10^{-5}$). The runtime advantage persists: 0.07~s per
locus for L1 versus 1.8~s for SuSiE.

The 27--2 headline is regime-specific, not universal. A 100-replicate
replication on 1000 Genomes chromosome~22 without tissue-specific
eQTL causal enrichment---$\beta = 0.2$, $h^2 = 0.02$, 30 rotating
centres across 17--46~Mb---converges to parity between L1 (54\%
rank-\#1) and SuSiE (57\%), with L1 retaining a $\sim$25$\times$
runtime advantage. The two results together bound the claim:
\textbf{L1's decisive statistical advantage emerges when annotation
informativeness is high} (tissue-specific eQTLs, moderate-impact
functional consequences, pathway co-enrichment); \textbf{under
uniform annotation conditions the advantage narrows to parity on
rank and persists only on speed.} This is the pattern that a
relational prior should produce, not a universally stronger
statistical test.

\subsection*{Head-to-head against state-of-the-art baselines}

A 2026 fine-mapping manuscript must compare against the most recent
state of the art\cite{wu2026genome}. We integrated all six methods
benchmarked by Wu~\emph{et~al.}~2026 into a common GraphGWAS
interface: SuSiE\cite{wang2020simple}, FINEMAP\cite{benner2016finemap},
SuSiE-inf\cite{cui2024improving}, FINEMAP-inf\cite{cui2024improving},
a Polyfun-proxy\cite{weissbrod2020polyfun} that feeds our eQTL
priors to SuSiE via \texttt{prior\_weights}, and
SBayesRC\cite{zheng2024leveraging}. On 30 1000 Genomes chromosome~22
F1 simulations at $h^2 = 0.05$ (Figure~\ref{fig:baselines};
full numerical breakdown in Supplementary Table~S2), SuSiE-inf
marginally improves on
SuSiE (22/30 vs 21/30 rank-\#1), consistent with Wu~\emph{et~al.}.
HBP reaches 20/30 rank-\#1---one below SuSiE---at 6--60$\times$ the
speed of every baseline (HBP~0.016~s; SuSiE-inf~0.16~s;
FINEMAP-inf~0.20~s; SuSiE~0.98~s; FINEMAP~2.2~s).

When the same eQTL prior we give HBP is passed to SuSiE via
Polyfun-proxy, SuSiE's rank-\#1 count rises from 10/20 to 11/20 on a
parallel weak-signal benchmark. L1 matches SuSiE~+~prior on
rank-\#1 (11/20) at 26$\times$ the speed (0.055~s vs 1.45~s).
Combined with the 27--2 result above, the head-to-head landscape
resolves: \textbf{graph-native priors and flat-vector priors converge
on accuracy when the prior information is identical; the graph
wins decisively on speed, and---when the graph captures
tissue-specific eQTL structure that the flat prior averages
out---also on accuracy.}

SBayesRC occupies a different problem. It is a genome-wide
multi-component Bayesian mixture processing $\sim$1.2~M HapMap~3
(HM3) SNPs in one Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) pass and requires
$N \gtrsim 10^5$. We verified the
SBayesRC pipeline end-to-end on the Wu lab canonical UKB example
(1{,}154{,}522 HM3 SNPs; 3{,}016 with PIP~$>$~0.5; 51~s total runtime)
but a like-for-like comparison on our 1KG region-specific simulations
is not statistically meaningful. HBP/L1 target high-precision
single-locus resolution at leads surfaced by genome-wide methods;
the two approaches are complementary within a workflow.

The 6--60$\times$ speed advantage of HBP over the Bayesian
baselines is partly a statistical benefit (fewer latent effects to
fit) and partly a data-access benefit of the underlying
computational substrate. Flat-prior methods could in principle
accept graph-structured priors, but operationalising this requires
reconstructing the multi-omics neighbourhood of each locus on
demand from separate files (BED tracks for regulatory elements,
GTF annotations for genes, TSV tables for pathways, TSV matrices
for tissue-specific eQTLs). Encoding the same information as a
graph database collapses that multi-step file join into a single
index-free-adjacency traversal that returns in milliseconds. The
relational prior is formally expressible without a graph-database
substrate, but at biobank-scale multi-omics coverage it is
computationally prohibitive without one. The accuracy--speed
frontier observed here is therefore a joint property of a new
method (HBP/L1) and a new data model (typed graph with
first-class labelled edges).

\subsection*{PIP calibration and null false-positive rate}

A fine-mapper must produce PIPs that mean what they say and must not
fire under the null (Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}). Across 100 null
simulations (no causal variant, Gaussian phenotype) on random 50~kb
yeast loci, \textbf{all four methods report PIP~$<$~0.5 for every
variant in every replicate}. Mean maximum PIP is 0.004 (L1),
0.003 (HBP), 0.029 (FINEMAP) and 0.013 (SuSiE); the softmax-based
bound $\mathbb{E}[\max_i \pi_i] \leq O(\sqrt{\log n}/n)$ derived in
Theorem~4 predicts $\leq$0.005 at $n = 5000$, matched empirically to
2\% accuracy. On 200 simulations across four heritability levels
($h^2 \in \{0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$), observed true-discovery
rates match the diagonal for FINEMAP, SuSiE and L1 within each PIP
bin, with slight conservative bias for L1 at mid-range PIPs. HBP
caps PIPs at $\approx$0.7 by design---softmax over a diffused prior
distributes mass across graph-related variants---but when HBP
assigns PIP~$>$~0.5 the variant is causal 99\% of the time. All four
methods are calibrated to the standards of the field.

\subsection*{Power scales with sample size}

To characterise how HBP's fine-mapping quality scales with $N$, we
subsampled 1000 Genomes Phase~3 chromosome~22 at
$N \in \{500, 1{,}000, 2{,}000, 3{,}000\}$ and ran 30 F1-style
simulations per $N$ (causal minor allele frequency (MAF)
10--40\%, $h^2 = 0.10$). Mean PIP
on the causal variant increases monotonically:
\textbf{0.54~$\rightarrow$~0.58~$\rightarrow$~0.59~$\rightarrow$~0.77}
(Figure~\ref{fig:power_vs_N}). At $N = 3{,}000$ (full 1KG), 73\% of
replicates place the causal variant at rank~1 and mean PIP
exceeds~0.77. The trend extrapolates linearly beyond the training
regime, supporting the claim that HBP's relational prior remains
well-behaved at biobank scale---a claim we test directly on
Pan-UK Biobank in the next subsection.

\subsection*{Cross-ancestry fine-mapping: 1KG simulated and Pan-UKB real-data}\label{sec:cross_anc}

\textbf{Controlled simulation on 1000 Genomes.} LD structure differs
substantially across ancestries, and many causal variants are
ancestry-private. We restricted 1KG chromosome~22 to the three
largest superpopulations (EUR $n = 503$, AFR $n = 661$,
EAS $n = 504$), simulated F1 loci with $h^2 = 0.05$ within each
ancestry (30 replicates), and ran HBP. The rank-\#1 ordering is
\textbf{AFR 53\% $>$ EAS 27\% $>$ EUR 17\%}, with mean PIPs
0.35, 0.21 and 0.08 respectively (Figure~\ref{fig:cross_anc}a).
This is the expected direction: AFR has older, less-correlated LD,
producing fewer indistinguishable proxies per locus. HBP's
precision holds across all three ancestries; it does not fall apart
on non-European LD, unlike methods that condition implicitly on
European summary-statistics reference panels.

\textbf{Real-biobank demonstration on Pan-UK Biobank.} We applied
the same HBP algorithm---through a sumstats-only entry path that
consumes Pan-UK Biobank\cite{karczewski2024pan,bycroft2018uk}
summary statistics streamed via \texttt{tabix}\cite{li2011tabix} over
HTTPS---to four canonical multi-ancestry loci:
\emph{FTO} (body mass index,
BMI\cite{frayling2007common}),
\emph{APOA5} (triglycerides\cite{do2013common}),
\emph{LDLR} (low-density lipoprotein cholesterol,
LDL-C\cite{teslovich2010biological}) and
\emph{HMGA2} (height\cite{weedon2008genome,yengo2022saturated}),
across the four Pan-UKB ancestries with $N > 2{,}500$
(EUR $N = 420{,}531$; CSA $8{,}876$; AFR $6{,}636$; EAS $2{,}709$).
Pan-UKB coordinates (GRCh37) were lifted to GRCh38 to align with our
1KG reference panel, from which ancestry-matched in-sample LD was
computed. On three of the four EUR cells (BMI, LDL, height) HBP
resolved a \textbf{single-variant 95\% credible set at
PIP~=~1.000}. The Height/HMGA2 signal is cross-ancestry robust:
CSA resolves to a single variant at PIP~=~1.000 at
$N = 8{,}876$ and EAS to a single variant at PIP~=~0.97 at
$N = 2{,}709$---a real-data confirmation of the power--vs--$N$
trend on a 145$\times$ smaller sample than EUR. Triglycerides/APOA5
shows two-ancestry convergence: EUR and CSA both top-rank the same
indel (11:116767975:A:AAAT) with PIP~=~0.51 and 0.73. Smaller-$N$
cells expand their credible sets to $\sim$600--1{,}000 variants as
statistically expected (Figure~\ref{fig:cross_anc}b). The same
codebase, the same algorithm, and no EUR-specific tuning were
required.

\subsection*{Cross-species portability: yeast and \emph{Arabidopsis}}

The same code fine-maps non-human organisms. We applied GraphGWAS
unchanged to the \emph{Arabidopsis thaliana} 1001 Genomes
Project\cite{alonso20161001} and the canonical flowering-time-at-
10$^{\circ}$C (FT10) phenotype\cite{andres2012genetic}
(1{,}003 accessions). On the $\pm$25~kb window around the top GWAS
signal at chr4:6{,}771{,}025 (1{,}353 MAF~$\geq$~2\% variants), HBP
converged in \textbf{0.096~s} and concentrated \textbf{98.9\% of
the posterior on chr4:6{,}771{,}025:T$\rightarrow$A}, returning a
credible set of size~1 that distinguishes the causal call from a
neighbour 368~bp away sharing most of the LD signal.
The yeast 1011-genome application\cite{peter2018genome,bloom2015genetic}
(35 growth traits on 971 strains after matching; full details in
\texttt{docs/YEAST\_ANALYSIS\_REPORT.md}) recovered established causal
genes---\emph{TUP1} and \emph{SPT7} for ethanol tolerance,
\emph{PPG1} and \emph{BMH2} for heat, \emph{GCR1} and \emph{HXT} loci
for galactose, \emph{YRR1} for caffeine---at 35--50 fine-mapping loci
across five traits.
The cross-species portability is not coincidental: the graph schema
is species-agnostic, and every species-specific component
(ortholog mapping, pathway database, regulatory annotation)
attaches as typed edges without algorithmic modification.

\subsection*{Whole-genome scan across 18 IRRI morphological traits}
\label{sec:irri_scan}

To test GraphGWAS at the scale its design targets --- many traits,
many loci, no curated ground truth --- we ran a whole-genome GWAS on
the 3{,}024-accession 3kRG panel (29.6~M biallelic SNPs) for 18
quantitative-like IRRI Standard Evaluation System traits extracted
from the published 3kRG phenotype
catalogue\cite{wang2018genomic} and linked to plink2 sample IDs via
the Wang~\emph{et al.}~2018 metadata (IRGC accession number
cross-reference). PLINK2 \texttt{--glm linear} with 10 genotype PCs
identified between 127 and 152{,}348 genome-wide significant
variants per trait ($\lambda_{GC}$: 0.50--2.07, median 1.09). We then
ran L1 and HBP fine-mapping on the top-3 genome-wide-significant plus
top-1 suggestive independent leads per trait (72 total) using
$\pm$250~kb windows and the rice multi-omics graph cache.

\paragraph{Ground-truth validation: fine-mapped leads recover textbook
rice causal genes.} We compared all 72 fine-mapped lead positions against
the 269-gene Ren \emph{et al.}~2023 catalogue\cite{ren2023rice} of curated
grain-quality causal genes (the same catalogue we used for the ablation
study, but applied here to the IRRI-scan output rather than the simulated
causals). \textbf{24 of 72 leads (33\%) lie within 250~kb of at least
one Ren-2023 gene}, with 14 of 18 traits contributing $\geq 1$ validated
locus. Notable hits include the textbook rice red-pericarp gene
\emph{Rc} (only \textbf{4.5~kb} from the SCCO\_REV Chr7:6.07~Mb lead),
\emph{TGW6} (12.3 kb from LLT\_CODE Chr6:25.11~Mb), the awn-development
gene \emph{GAD1/RAE2} (4.4 kb from AWPR\_REPRO Chr8:23.98~Mb,
$p\!=\!4.4\!\times\!10^{-90}$, L1 PIP$\,=\,$0.999), grain-shape genes
\emph{An-1} (240 kb from BLCO\_REV\_VEG Chr4:16.47~Mb, L1 PIP$=$1.00) and
\emph{BG1} (68.7 kb from SPKF Chr3:4.10~Mb), the panicle-development
gene \emph{OsER1} (62 kb from APCO\_REV\_REPRO Chr6:5.31~Mb, L1
PIP$=$0.77), and the Vp1-orthologue \emph{OsVP1} (176 kb from PTY
Chr1:39.54~Mb). The two highest-confidence pigment-related loci
--- APCO\_REV\_REPRO Chr10:13.32~Mb (L1 PIP$=$0.999, 28.5 kb from
\emph{BRD2/LTBSG1}) and SCCO\_REV Chr7:6.07~Mb (4.5 kb from
\emph{Rc}) --- both validate against well-characterised pigment
biology, suggesting the IRRI ordinal pigment-trait scoring really does
capture the same axis of variation as the molecular literature.
Per-trait validation rates reach 100\% (4/4) for APCO\_REV\_REPRO and
$\geq 50$\% for BLCO\_REV\_VEG, LPPUB, SCCO\_REV, SPKF, and PEX\_REPRO;
4 traits (AUCO\_REV\_VEG, CUST\_REPRO, LA, LPCO\_REV\_POST) had no
fine-mapped lead within 250~kb of a Ren-2023 gene.

\paragraph{Single-variant credible-set resolution.}
Across the 72 leads fine-mapped: \textbf{L1 delivers a
single-variant 95\% credible set on 11 loci (15\%)} with
top-variant PIP $\geq 0.99$; HBP delivers the same on 8 loci (11\%);
both methods agree on the same single variant in 8 loci (11\%). HBP
produces a tighter CS than L1 on 14 loci (19\%); L1 wins on 10 loci
(14\%); the two agree exactly on the remaining 48 loci (67\%). The
8 doubly-resolved loci --- 3 in AUCO\_REV\_VEG (culm colour) at
Chr11:3.96~Mb, Chr2:13.24~Mb and Chr12:11.57~Mb, 2 in AWCO\_REV
(awn colour) at Chr8:27.20~Mb and Chr8:17.14~Mb, 2 in LIGCO\_REV\_VEG
(ligule colour) at Chr11:14.60~Mb and Chr4:1.39~Mb, plus SCCO\_REV
Chr11:22.60~Mb --- are the most confident fine-mapping calls on the
entire panel.

\paragraph{When L1 outruns HBP, and vice versa.} The IRRI scan is
a mirror of the ablation finding (\S\ref{sec:ablation}). On strong,
clean-signal loci (e.g. AWCO\_REV Chr8:27.20~Mb, $p = 2.7\times10^{-26}$),
LD deconvolution alone pinpoints the causal: L1 CS $=$ 1, PIP $=$ 1.00,
and HBP agrees. On loci where the sumstats leave ambiguity and the
multi-omics cache has locus-specific heterogeneity, HBP narrows CS
further; on loci where the cache is uniformly dense (e.g. APCO\_REV\_REPRO
Chr10:13.32~Mb: L1 CS $=$ 1, HBP CS $=$ 478), the graph prior
\emph{widens} the CS, exactly as the rice-ablation \emph{resolvable} vs
\emph{unresolvable} dichotomy predicts. The practical implication
for platform users is: run L1 first for tight CS at high-signal loci,
add HBP when the graph cache has discriminative biological structure
at the target locus.

\paragraph{Population-structure caveat.} Four traits
(APCO\_REV\_REPRO, CUST\_REPRO, SCCO\_REV, CUAN\_REPRO) have
$\lambda_{GC} \in [1.6, 2.1]$, indicating some residual
subpopulation confounding (the 3kRG panel spans $F_{ST} \approx 0.5$
between XI and GJ subpopulations under 10-PC correction). Lead-loci
counts on these traits should be interpreted as enriched for
stratification artefacts, and CS size at their top leads should be
read conservatively. The eight doubly-resolved loci above are all
in well-calibrated traits ($\lambda_{GC} \in [0.50, 1.38]$).

\subsection*{Ablation: when does multi-omics coverage help fine-mapping?}
\label{sec:ablation}

A common implicit claim in annotated fine-mapping is that more
functional coverage uniformly sharpens credible sets. We tested
this directly on the 3{,}000 Rice Genomes panel, where we have real
grain-quality GWAS sumstats and curated ground-truth causal genes
(Ren \emph{et al.}~2023\cite{ren2023rice}). We randomly subsampled
the rice multi-omics cache (RAP-DB variant$\to$gene +
Ren-2023 gene$\to$pathway + RicePPINet gene$\leftrightarrow$gene
PPI at Prob~$\geq$~0.7) at five retention fractions
(10\%, 25\%, 50\%, 75\%, 100\%) $\times$ 10 bootstrap seeds, and
re-ran HBP on each of five simulated grain-quality causal loci
(GW2, BG1, GS5, TGW6, GW8/OsSPL16).

The effect of multi-omics coverage is \emph{not} uniform --- it
depends sharply on the locus's signal-to-noise regime
(Figure~\ref{fig:ablation}). We observed three regimes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Resolvable} (baseline PIP $\geq$ 0.01): GW8/OsSPL16
  sits here. HBP is already confident in the causal under sumstats +
  LD alone; adding more annotation has no visible effect
  (rank 2 at every coverage level).
  \item \textbf{Borderline} (baseline PIP $\sim$ 10$^{-3}$): BG1 and
  TGW6 sit here. \textbf{These are the loci where multi-omics coverage
  matters most.} BG1's causal variant rank improves from 154 at 10\%
  coverage to 4 at 100\% (a \textbf{38.6-fold} gain); TGW6's rank
  improves from 65 to 3 (\textbf{21.6-fold}). These are the regimes
  where LD confusers compete with the true causal and the relational
  prior acts as a tiebreaker.
  \item \textbf{Underpowered} (baseline PIP $\leq$ 10$^{-4}$): GS5
  and GW2 sit here. The sumstats alone cannot support the causal;
  adding annotation mass shifts probability onto other (uninformative)
  variants, so the true-causal rank \emph{degrades} with more coverage
  (GS5: 514$\to$1139; GW2: essentially flat at rank $\approx$10{,}900).
\end{itemize}

Two further observations emerged. First, annotation \emph{heterogeneity}
matters more than density. On human chromosome~22 with GTEx~+~STRING
(near-uniform 1 eGene + $N$ PPI partners per annotated variant), HBP
output was identical across all 10 bootstrap seeds at every coverage
fraction (rank\_std $=$ 0 for every locus tested). Random edge-dropping
preserves the homogeneous graph structure, so the posterior does not
change. Rice, with hand-curated Ren-2023 pathway labels on 269 genes
alongside RicePPINet PPIs, shows real per-seed variance (rank\_std
7--18 at borderline/unresolved loci), because surviving subsets
emphasise different biological relationships. Second, the credible-set
size for resolvable and borderline loci is coverage-insensitive above
25\% retention (BG1, GS5, TGW6 all plateau immediately), but under-resolved
loci inflate linearly with coverage (GW2: 901~$\to$~1{,}865) --- a
second signature of the prior adding uniform mass rather than discriminative
information in this regime. Full per-locus traces are provided in
Supplementary Note~S4 and reproducible via
\texttt{tests/multiomics\_ablation\_rice.py}.

\subsection*{Cross-species replication: 4 species, 70 traits, 383 loci}
\label{sec:multispecies}

To stress-test the GraphGWAS pipeline beyond the rice 3kRG study,
we replicated the same end-to-end workflow ---
build species-specific multi-omics graph cache, GWAS or load published
sumstats, fine-map all genome-wide-significant lead loci, validate against
literature ground truth --- on three additional organisms with very
different genome architectures, sample sizes, and annotation densities
(Table~\ref{tab:multispecies}).

\paragraph{Yeast (1011 genome panel, 35 growth traits).}
We built a variant--gene--GO\_slim cache from SGD features and the GO
slim mapping (1.14~M variants, 60\% of the panel; no PPI added to v1).
Across 35 grammar-corrected GWAS sumstats files, GraphGWAS fine-mapped
245 leads (top-3 GW-sig + top-1 suggestive per trait, $\pm$15~kb
windows). Yeast LD is so strong that no locus reaches L1 CS$=$1, but
HBP narrows the credible set on \textbf{244/245 loci (100\%)} relative
to L1, with 5/245 reaching L1 PIP$\geq 0.5$. 44/245 leads (18\%) lie
within 250~kb of a literature-curated trait-relevant gene
(Bloom 2015, Peter 2018; e.g.\ TUP1/SPT7 for ethanol, CUP1/CUP2 for
copper, GAL pathway for galactose, ENA1/HOG1 for salt, ERG/PDR for
fluconazole).

\paragraph{Arabidopsis (1001 Genomes, 14 priority traits).}
We assembled a TAIR10 + Plant Reactome + STRING-Ath graph cache covering
285{,}830 variants across the 5 chromosomes, then ran a multi-trait
plink2 GWAS on 14 priority phenotypes (the 11 AraGWAS-bonferroni-validated
traits + FT10/FT16/FLC). Of the 54 fine-mapped leads,
\textbf{3 reach L1 CS$=$1 with PIP$\geq 0.98$} (PH20\_Storage7days at
chr5:9.93~Mb, chr5:13.55~Mb, and chr4:9.52~Mb), and HBP narrows the
credible set on 29/54 (54\%) loci --- the highest "graph-helps" rate
of all four species. Validation against AraGWAS bonferroni hits +
literature-canonical flowering genes (FRI, FLC, CONSTANS, GIGANTEA,
VIN3, FT, DOG1, HKT1) gives 3/54 (6\%); the lower rate reflects the
chr-4-only AraGWAS file rather than poor fine-mapping (the 3 CS$=$1 hits
on chr4-9 and chr5-9-and-13 are far from the chr4 AraGWAS region).

\paragraph{Human (Pan-UKB, chr22 across 4 phenotypes).}
We used tabix-over-HTTPS to fetch chr22 sumstats from Pan-UKB for BMI,
Height, LDL, and Triglycerides (b37 → b38 liftover via pyliftover),
clustered GW-sig leads at 500~kb, and fine-mapped the top-5 per trait
using the chr22 GTEx + STRING graph cache. 12 leads were fine-mapped
across 3 traits (Height returned 0 GW-sig variants on chr22 from
Pan-UKB EUR after liftover, leaving BMI, LDL, TG); 4/12 (33\%)
overlap a literature-canonical chr22 locus (APOL1/APOL2 for LDL,
PLA2G6/SREBF2/PPARA for TG and Height). \textbf{L1 and HBP produced
exactly identical credible sets and top-PIP variants on every locus},
consistent with the rice ablation finding: when the multi-omics cache
is uniform per variant (1 GTEx eGene + $\sim 20$ STRING partners), HBP
cannot add discriminative information beyond LD-deconvolved sumstats.

\paragraph{Synthesis.} The four-species replication confirms the
ablation diagnosis: \emph{the value HBP adds beyond L1 depends not on
annotation density but on per-variant heterogeneity in the prior.}
Yeast (GO\_slim, sparse) and Arabidopsis (Plant Reactome, sparse)
show 100\% and 54\% HBP-tighter rates; rice (Ren-2023 + RicePPINet,
sparse) shows 19\%; the dense-and-uniform human chr22 GTEx+STRING
cache shows 0\%. Single-variant resolution rates correlate with
sample size as expected (rice~3{,}024 → 15\% L1~CS$=$1; arabidopsis~1{,}135
→ 6\%; yeast~971 → 0\% but 2\% PIP$\geq 0.5$; human chr22 with effective
N$\sim$420{,}000 but limited to 6 leads → 0\% CS$=$1 within the chr22
slice). The GraphGWAS pipeline runs unchanged across all four species;
the only species-specific component is the loaded annotation graph,
exactly as the cross-species portability claim predicts.

\subsection*{Method selection and extensions}

Different questions call for different methods (Figure~\ref{fig:selection};
full decision table in Supplementary Table~S3). All GraphGWAS methods share a unified
input interface---graph database, BGEN file, or Pan-UKB-style
summary statistics---and a unified output:
\texttt{AssociationResult} and \texttt{CredibleSet} typed graph
nodes queryable by the database's native query language alongside
the biological annotations that produced them. This is the
practical realisation of the graph-native claim: the fine-mapping
output is itself a graph object, not a decorated table, and the
downstream question ``which credible-set variants are in a
druggable pathway?'' is a single traversal rather than a multi-step
enrichment cascade.

The same graph substrate extends naturally beyond single-variant
fine-mapping. As a brief preview of forthcoming work, LD-pruned
co-occurrence (M1) reformulates pairwise epistasis discovery as a
search over a maximal $r^2 < \tau$ independent set of the LD graph,
testing only pairs within it. Theorem~1 (Supplementary Note~S1)
proves the search space shrinks to $\approx 1/k(\tau)^2$ of the
exhaustive $M(M-1)/2$ pairs; on 1000 Genomes chromosome~22
($M = 102{,}467$, $\tau = 0.5$), $k(\tau) \approx 0.003$ yields a
\textbf{42{,}000$\times$ search-space reduction}
($5.2 \times 10^9 \rightarrow \approx 31$~K candidate pairs) with
no loss of ground-truth interaction detection
(Supplementary Fig.~S2). Full epistasis benchmarking is the subject
of a companion manuscript in preparation.
