\abstract{Linkage disequilibrium makes causal GWAS variants indistinguishable from correlated neighbours --- the fine-mapping problem. The challenge is species-specific: human cohorts have high signal but dense, ancestry-imbalanced LD; yeast and \emph{Arabidopsis} have exceptionally long LD; crop germplasm such as the 3{,}000 Rice Genomes panel spans large between-population $F_{ST}$ with mostly ordinal phenotypes. Bayesian fine-mappers integrate annotations as flat per-variant priors, discarding the relational structure linking variants to tissue-specific eQTLs, pathways and protein--protein interactions. Hierarchical belief propagation (HBP) on a variant--gene--pathway factor graph with PPI coupling matches Bayesian baselines at 6--60$\times$ speed; an annotation-adaptive complement (L1) wins 27--2 against SuSiE at weak signal with tissue-specific eQTL priors. The same code fine-maps Pan-UK Biobank across four ancestries (recovering canonical lipid genes \emph{LDLR}, \emph{APOE}, \emph{LPL}, \emph{GCKR}, \emph{ANGPTL3} at single-variant resolution), yeast, \emph{Arabidopsis} and rice. An intervention experiment across 692 leads establishes that per-variant heterogeneity of the prior, not annotation density, is the operative quantity: adding a regulatory-element flag to an otherwise uniform human cache flips HBP narrower than L1 from 0\% to 88\% of leads.}
